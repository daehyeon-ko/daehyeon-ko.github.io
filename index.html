Profile

My research interests span micro/nanoscale fabrication, advanced materials, and electronic devices. I am particularly interested in how materials synthesis, processing, and structural design influence material properties and device performance, and how these relationships can guide the development of functional micro/nanosystems.


My previous research includes metal nanoparticle synthesis, conductive ink development, laser-assisted fabrication of flexible electrodes, and microelectrode patterning on curved microfibers for single-fiber electronics. These experiences have shaped my broader interest in connecting materials development with device fabrication, characterization, and integration. Building on this background, I aim to explore new materials and fabrication approaches for electronic, optical, and electromechanical devices.

Research

Spatially Programmable Microelectrode Patterning on Highly Curved Microfibers for Single-Fiber Electronics (M.S. Research Project)

Research Summary

Developed a mask-free laser-writing method to form silver microelectrodes on highly curved nylon microfibers. Localized photothermal activation enables spatially selective patterning, with control over electrode geometry and electrical properties. The electrodes combine mechanical robustness with compatibility for device integration. Demonstrations of passive circuits and multicolor micro-LEDs establish a single-fiber platform for programmable electronics on curved, flexible surfaces.

Metal Nanoparticles Synthesis and Flexible Electrode Fabrication (Undergraduate Research Project)

Research Summary

Developed a facile synthesis approach for nickel- and copper-based nanoparticles and formulated conductive inks for flexible electrode fabrication. Laser processing converts the deposited materials into conductive patterns, connecting solution-based material preparation with electrode manufacturing. The project brings together scalable nanoparticle synthesis, conductive ink development, and laser-based fabrication as a materials-to-device approach for flexible electronics.
